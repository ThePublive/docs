This page defines the metrics and fields that you see on the [Prompts](/axp/monitor/track-prompts), [prompt analytics](/axp/monitor/prompt-analytics), [Sources](/axp/monitor/sources), and [Brand Visibility](/axp/monitor/brand-visibility) pages.

## Key terms

The formulas on this page use these terms.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Response** | What one platform returns for one prompt, at one time. Each tracked prompt gets one response from each of its platforms every 12 hours. |
| **Answered response** | A response in which the platform answered the prompt. If the platform did not answer, AXP does not include the response in any percentage or average. |
| **Mention** | One occurrence of a brand in a response. One response can mention a brand more than one time. |
| **Citation** | A URL that the platform gives as a source for its response. AXP counts each URL one time per response, also if the platform lists it more than once. AXP keeps up to 25 citations for each response. |
| **Inline citation** | A link that the platform puts in the response text. Each URL that the platform lists as a source counts as at least one inline citation. This is true also if the response text does not link to it. |

<Info>
AXP compares URLs in a standard form. It removes `www.` and tracking parameters, for example `?utm_source=openai`. So `https://www.example.com/page?utm_source=openai` and `https://example.com/page` count as the same URL.
</Info>

## Metrics

| Metric | What it measures | Better when |
|---|---|---|
| [Visibility](#visibility) | How often the responses mention your brand | Higher |
| [Share of voice](#share-of-voice) | Your part of all brand mentions | Higher |
| [Position](#position) | How early the responses mention your brand | Lower |
| [Sentiment](#sentiment) | How positive the responses are about your brand | Higher |
| [Citations](#citations) | How many times the responses cite a domain, a URL, or your brand's domains | Higher, for your own domains |
| [Citation rate](#citation-rate) | How many inline citations the responses contain | Higher, for your own domains |
| [Web search](#web-search) | How often the platform searches the web before it answers | No good or bad value |
| [Mentions](#mentions) | Which brands the responses mention | More detail below |
| [Volume](#volume) | How much people search for the prompt | Higher, for prompts worth tracking |

### Example data

The sections below use this example. One prompt has 12 responses in the date range. The platform did not answer 2 of them, so the prompt has 10 answered responses.

| Data in the 10 answered responses | Value |
|---|---|
| Responses that mention your brand | 4 |
| Mentions of your brand | 6 |
| Mentions of Competitor A | 12 |
| Mentions of Competitor B | 2 |
| Your position in the 4 responses that mention your brand | 1, 2, 1, 4 |
| Sentiment about your brand in those 4 responses | +0.6, +0.2, +0.4, −0.2 |
| Inline citations in all 10 responses | 15 |
| Citations of your domain | 3 |
| Responses in which the platform searched the web | 8 |

### Visibility

Visibility is the percentage of answered responses that mention your brand. It shows how often the platforms mention your brand when they answer the prompts that you track.

```text
Visibility = (answered responses that mention your brand ÷ answered responses) × 100
```

In the example, 4 of the 10 answered responses mention your brand. Visibility is `4 ÷ 10 × 100 = 40%`. The 2 unanswered responses do not count.

- A response counts one time, also if it mentions your brand many times. To count each mention, use [Share of voice](#share-of-voice).
- On the **Tracking** tab, the **Brand Visibility** column shows visibility for each prompt. On the **Brand Visibility** page, the **AI visibility** card shows it for all your prompts together.
- The **Brand Visibility** page uses only prompts that do not mention your brand. A prompt that mentions your brand almost always gets a response that mentions your brand.

### Share of voice

Share of voice is your brand's part of all brand mentions. It shows how much of the conversation your brand gets compared with every other brand that the responses mention.

```text
Share of voice = (mentions of your brand ÷ mentions of all brands) × 100
```

In the example, the responses mention your brand 6 times, Competitor A 12 times, and Competitor B 2 times. If this is your only prompt, share of voice is `6 ÷ (6 + 12 + 2) × 100 = 30%`.

- Share of voice counts mentions, not responses. A response that mentions your brand 3 times adds 3.
- For one prompt or one topic, the bottom of the formula is the mentions of all brands in the responses to **all** your prompts. So the share of voice of all your prompts adds up to the share of voice of your brand on the **Brand Visibility** page.
- Visibility and share of voice can move in different directions. More responses can mention your brand (higher visibility) while competitors get even more mentions (lower share of voice).

### Position

Position is the average rank of your brand among the brands that a response mentions. Rank 1 means that the response mentions your brand before every other brand.

```text
Position = sum of your rank in each response that mentions your brand ÷ number of those responses
```

In the example, your ranks in the 4 responses are 1, 2, 1, and 4. Position is `(1 + 2 + 1 + 4) ÷ 4 = 2.0`. The dashboard shows it as `#2.0`.

- Lower is better. A decrease in position is an improvement.
- Position uses only the responses that mention your brand. A response that does not mention your brand does not make your position worse. That response shows "–" in the **Position** column of the **Prompt responses** table. To see how often your brand is missing, use [Visibility](#visibility).
- The rank includes every brand that the response mentions, not only your competitors.
- In a chart over time, the position for each day is the average of all responses on that day.

### Sentiment

Sentiment shows how positive the responses are about your brand. AXP reads each response that mentions your brand and gives your brand a score from −1 (very negative) to +1 (very positive). The dashboard changes the average score to a scale from 0 to 100.

```text
Sentiment = (average score in the responses that mention your brand + 1) × 50
```

In the example, the scores are +0.6, +0.2, +0.4, and −0.2. The average is `0.25`. Sentiment is `(0.25 + 1) × 50 = 62.5`.

- 50 is neutral. A value above 50 is positive. A value below 50 is negative.
- Sentiment uses only the responses that mention your brand.
- Where space is small, the dashboard shows a label: **Positive**, **Neutral**, or **Negative**.
- Sentiment is a score, not a percentage. "60% of responses were positive" is a useful statement. "Sentiment = 60%" is not.

### Citations

Citations count how many times the responses cite a source. What AXP counts depends on where you see the metric.

| Where | What AXP counts |
|---|---|
| A prompt on the **Tracking** tab, or a brand | The citations of the domains of that brand |
| A domain on the **Sources** pages | The citations of all URLs on that domain |
| A URL on the **Sources** pages | The citations of that URL |
| One response | The inline citations in that response |

In the example, the responses cite your domain 3 times. The **Citations** column for the prompt shows `3`.

- AXP counts a URL one time per response. A response that cites 2 different URLs on one domain adds 2 to that domain.
- A citation does not mean a mention. A platform can cite your page and not mention your brand. To check, open the [URL details](/axp/monitor/sources#url-details).

### Citation rate

Citation rate is an average number of inline citations. It shows how much the platform supports its response with links. It also shows how often the platform links to a source in the response text. The bottom of the formula depends on where you see the metric.

| Where | Formula |
|---|---|
| A prompt on the **Tracking** tab | Inline citations in the answered responses ÷ answered responses |
| A brand | Inline citations of the brand's domains ÷ answered responses |
| A domain | Inline citations of URLs on the domain ÷ responses that use a URL on the domain as a source |
| A URL | Inline citations of the URL ÷ responses that use the URL as a source |
| One response | Inline citations ÷ different URLs that the response cites |

In the example, the 10 answered responses contain 15 inline citations. The citation rate of the prompt is `15 ÷ 10 = 1.5`.

For a domain, look at `example.com`. 5 responses use a page on `example.com` as a source. These responses contain 8 inline citations of it. The citation rate of `example.com` is `8 ÷ 5 = 1.6`.

- Citation rate is a plain number, not a percentage.
- A source with many citations but a low citation rate gives the platform background information. The platform seldom links to it in the text.

### Web search

Web search is the percentage of responses in which the platform searched the web before it answered.

```text
Web search = (responses with at least one web search ÷ responses with search data) × 100
```

In the example, the platform searched the web in 8 of the 10 responses. Web search is `8 ÷ 10 × 100 = 80%`.

- A low value means that the platform answers the prompt mostly from its own knowledge. Such responses often have no citations.
- Some responses do not report whether the platform searched. AXP does not include these responses. It does not count them as 0.
- Web search has no good or bad value. It tells you if the platform answers this prompt from the web, where your pages can influence the response.

### Mentions

On the **Tracking** tab, **Mentions** shows the brands that the responses to the prompt mention, as logos. `+5` means 5 more brands. When you sort by **Mentions**, AXP uses the number of different brands.

On the **Brand Visibility** page and in the response tables, mentions are the number of times the responses mention a brand. This is the same count that [Share of voice](#share-of-voice) uses.

In the example, the responses mention 3 different brands: your brand, Competitor A, and Competitor B. The **Tracking** tab shows 3 logos.

### Volume

Volume is the estimated search volume of the prompt. Use it to compare prompts and to decide which prompts are worth tracking. "–" means that no estimate is available for the prompt.

## Changes and trends

Next to a metric, a small green or red value shows the change from the previous period. On the Sources pages, an arrow shows the same change: up, down, or flat.

A change compares the selected date range with the period of the same length just before it. For example, a 30-day range compares with the 30 days before it. For **Position**, a decrease is an improvement.

## Prompt fields

These fields are on the **Tracking**, **Suggested**, and **Paused** tabs, and in the forms to add and edit a prompt.

| Field | What it means | Values |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompts** | The prompt text. You cannot change it after you create the prompt. | Free text |
| **Topic** | The group that the prompt belongs to. Each prompt has one topic. | A topic name |
| **Platforms** | The AI platforms that run the prompt | **ChatGPT**, **Gemini**. If you select no platform, AXP runs the prompt on all platforms. |
| **Location** | The market that the prompt targets. The location changes the sources that the AI finds when it searches the web. It does not change the prompt text. | Free text, for example `India` or `Delhi, India` |
| **Intent** | The goal of the buyer who asks the prompt. AXP sets it automatically. You cannot change it. | Refer to [Intent values](#intent-values) |
| **Branding** | Whether the prompt text mentions your brand. AXP sets this value from the prompt text. | **Branded**, **Non branded** |
| **Source** (**Origin** on the **Paused** tab) | How the prompt got into your list | **Manual**, **Suggested**, **Bulk**. Refer to [Prompt sources](/axp/monitor/overview#prompt-sources). |
| **Reason** | Why AXP suggested the prompt. It can also show the search volume of the keyword, for example "Decision gap · vol 2.4k", or the page URL. | Free text that AXP writes |
| **Date added** | The date that a member added the prompt, or accepted it from a suggestion | A date |

### Intent values

| Value | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | The buyer wants to learn about a subject. | "How does a personal loan work?" |
| **Commercial** | The buyer compares options before a purchase. | "Best personal loan apps in India" |
| **Transactional** | The buyer is ready to act or buy. | "Apply for a personal loan online" |

## Response fields

These fields are in the **Prompt responses** tables and in the **Prompt response** view.

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Response** | The start of the response text. Open the response to see the full text. |
| **Platform** | The AI platform that gave the response |
| **Mentions** | The brands that the response mentions, with your brand marked **(You)** in the response view |
| **Sources** | The domains or URLs that the response cites |
| **Position** | The rank of your brand in this response. "–" means that the response does not mention your brand. |
| **Citations** | The number of inline citations in this response |
| **Citation rate** | The number of inline citations in this response, divided by the number of different URLs that it cites |
| **Response details** | The date and time that AXP collected the response, and the location that the prompt used |

A response can show that AXP did not analyze it yet. This is different from a response that does not mention your brand.

## Source fields

These fields are on the [Sources](/axp/monitor/sources) pages.

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Domain type** | The type of website. AXP sets it automatically. Refer to [Domain types](#domain-types). |
| **URL type** | The type of page. AXP sets it automatically. Refer to [URL types](#url-types). |
| **Ownership** | Who controls the page. Refer to [Ownership](#ownership). |
| **Your brand mentioned** | **Yes** if the page content mentions your brand. **No** if it does not. |
| **Mentions** | On a URL: the brands that the page content mentions. On a domain: the brands that the pages on the domain mention. |
| **Position** (in **Brands mentioned**) | The order in which the page mentions the brand. `#1` means that the page mentions this brand first. |
| **Frequency** (in **Brands mentioned**) | The number of times the page mentions the brand |

These values come from the content of the cited page, not from the AI response. AXP reads the page in the background. Until it reads the page, these fields show "–".

### Domain types

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **You** | One of your own domains, from your Brand Profile |
| **Corporate** | The website of a company |
| **UGC** | A site with user-generated content, for example a forum, a review site, or a social platform |
| **Institutional** | The website of an institution, for example a government body, a regulator, or a university |

AXP can show other domain types when a domain does not fit these values.

### URL types

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Homepage** | The main page of a website |
| **Profile** | A page about one company, brand, or person, for example a company profile on a directory or a review site |
| **Other** | All other pages, for example articles, blog posts, and product pages |

### Ownership

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Owned** | The page is on one of the domains in your Brand Profile. |
| **Controlled** | The page is not on your domain, but your brand controls its content, for example your company profile on another site. |
| **Third Party** | Your brand does not control the page. |

## Things to know

- **Brands are not limited to your competitor list.** AXP counts every brand that a response mentions. This shows you which brands win the responses, including brands that you do not track.
- **A citation does not mean a mention.** An AI can cite a page that does not mention your brand. The [URL details](/axp/monitor/sources#url-details) page shows if the page mentions your brand.
- **A response without citations is valid.** When the AI does not search the web, the response has no citations.
- **Changes apply from the date of the change.** If you change the platforms or location of a prompt, past responses keep their values.
- **Old responses can have no value for new metrics.** AXP does not include these responses in averages. It does not count them as 0.
