**Google Discover Headline** is an opt-in field in Post Details, positioned directly below **Title**. Once enabled for your account, it carries the same data and purpose as the OG Title field you may already know — just renamed and relocated so it's visible while you write, instead of tucked inside SEO Properties.

## Enabling it

Google Discover Headline is opt-in — [contact us](mailto:support@thepublive.com) to have it enabled for your account. There's no setup required beyond that request.

<Note>
  If you don't request this, nothing changes in your dashboard. OG Title stays exactly where it is today, in SEO Properties, working exactly as it always has.
</Note>

## What changes

Once enabled, your existing OG Title value carries over into the new field automatically — nothing to re-enter. The OG Title input is then removed from the SEO Properties panel; every other field there (Meta Title, OG Description, Twitter Title) stays exactly as it was.

## Why it matters

Google Discover Headline is the strongest signal you can give Google for the headline shown when your content appears in Google Discover — though not a guarantee of what displays, since Google's own systems make the final call. It's a distinct field from your page's Title (H1) and your Meta Title, which serve separate jobs. Grouped inside SEO Properties alongside several similarly named fields, that distinction was easy to miss.

[Google's own Discover documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover) calls out compelling, non-misleading titles as a factor in whether content gets picked up by the feed, which is why this field now has a dedicated, visible spot next to Title. If left blank, Discover falls back to your page's Title instead.

## Using the field

- Hover the **ⓘ** icon on either **Title** or **Google Discover Headline** for a quick explanation of how each is used.
- If left blank, Google Discover shows your **Title** instead.
- A live character counter shows as you type. We recommend keeping it under approximately 70 characters so it displays cleanly in Discover — there's no hard limit, and longer headlines still save.
- The AI-generate (sparkle) icon next to the field works the same way it does on Title.

## Scope

Google Discover Headline is available on every content type that previously supported OG Title, including Template Pages. It's not available on Custom Content → Blank Page.

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Will I lose my existing OG Title value if I enable this?">
    No. Once enabled, your existing OG Title value is carried over into the new Google Discover Headline field automatically — nothing to re-enter or re-save.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does this affect my Meta Title, Twitter Title, or my page's H1?">
    No, whether or not you enable this. Google Discover Headline only controls what was previously OG Title. Meta Title, Twitter Title, structured data, and your page's H1 are all untouched.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will Google always show exactly the headline I set here?">
    Not necessarily, and that's expected. Google Discover Headline is the signal you send to Google for the feed, but Google's own systems make the final decision on what to display. A few ordinary reasons the two might not match:

    - **Indexing lag.** Google has to recrawl your page before a headline change appears in Discover — this can take hours to days, not seconds.
    - **Google's own choice.** Discover can pull from the page Title, the on-page H1, or occasionally a headline it generates itself, if its systems judge that to be a better fit for the feed.
    - **Caching.** What you see in your own Discover feed can be a cached, older version of the page.

    If you've set the field and reopened the post to confirm it saved correctly, you've done everything on your end — the display choice from that point is Google's.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does this affect my live pages, SEO, or existing traffic?">
    No. The underlying value is unchanged and there's no data migration, so nothing about how your live pages render or get indexed changes. This only affects where you find and edit the field in the editor.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="I can't find OG Title in SEO Properties anymore — is it gone?">
    OG Title only moves out of SEO Properties once Google Discover Headline is enabled for your account. If you haven't requested it, OG Title is exactly where it's always been.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does this affect my social share previews, like Facebook or LinkedIn?">
    Yes, for Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, and other Open Graph-based platforms — because Google Discover Headline is the same field as your OG Title, just renamed and relocated. Whatever you set here is what shows in those share-card headlines too.

    It doesn't affect Twitter/X previews, which read a separate Twitter Title field, or your Meta Title, structured data, and page H1 — those are all untouched.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a recommended format for a Discover Headline, beyond the character count?">
    No hard rule on length, structure, dates, or numbers — the character counter is a soft guide only, and longer headlines still save.

    The one steer worth keeping in mind: aim for curiosity and emotion over keyword-heavy phrasing. That's what tends to perform better in Discover, versus the clearer, keyword-led style that works for Search. For general guidance on what Google rewards in Discover, see [Google's own Discover documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="If we enable this and later change our mind, can we turn it off?">
    Yes. Since enabling is opt-in, disabling works the same way — [contact us](mailto:support@thepublive.com) and we'll switch it off for you.

    Turning it off doesn't touch your existing titles, OG data, or SEO setup. The field just moves back to its original spot in SEO Properties, exactly as it was before you enabled it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is there a report showing which of our posts have a Discover Headline set?">

  </Accordion>
