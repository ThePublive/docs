Use forms to collect data from your readers, for example contact requests, sign-ups, or applications. You define the form in Publive DXP. Your website shows the form and sends each response back to Publive DXP.

## What a form contains

| Part | What it does |
| ---- | ------------ |
| Fields | Each field has a type, such as short text, long text, email, number, true or false, or media. |
| Labels and help text | Each field can have a label, a tooltip, and placeholder text. |
| Validation | Each field can have rules, such as **required**, that a response must pass. |
| Groups | Put related fields together in a group. |
| Captcha | Protects the form against automated spam submissions. |

## Manage forms in the dashboard

- **Send responders a copy:** In **Form Settings**, turn on **Send responder a copy of their response**. Each person who submits the form then gets an email with their answers.
- **Control who can work with forms:** Roles have separate permissions to view, create, and manage forms and form submissions.
- **Track changes:** [Audit logs](/dxp/documentation/concepts-features/audit-logs) record when a member creates, updates, or deletes a form. To see only these records, filter by the **Form** resource type.

## Add a form to your website

A developer connects the form to your website. There are two ways to do this:

- **Iframe:** The fastest setup. Your page loads a form that Publive DXP hosts, inside an iframe.
- **Components:** Your website renders the form from its schema. Use this when you need a custom design, hidden fields, or control of the layout.

Start with the iframe unless you already know that you need custom rendering.
